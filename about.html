About this project

A domain-adaptation practical: train a headline topic classifier on general AG News titles, evaluate on a frozen UK BBC test set, then fine-tune on UK data and measure the lift — all on a Mac Studio with Apple Silicon MPS.

Goal Mac Studio Model Architecture Fine-tuning Data & labels Pipeline Evaluation Caveats

What we’re asking

Can a classifier trained on broad, mostly US-centric news titles transfer to UK BBC headlines — and does continued fine-tuning on UK text close the gap?

We compare five systems on the same frozen UK test set: Naive Bayes @ AG, DistilBERT @ AG, Naive Bayes @ UK, DistilBERT UK-only (no AG), and DistilBERT AG→UK continued fine-tuning. Charts live on the classification page.

Hardware: Mac Studio

Training and inference ran locally on Apple Silicon — not a cloud GPU box. DistilBERT is small enough that this machine is more than enough; the practical constraint was getting PyTorch’s MPS backend working cleanly.

MPS notes: we avoid fp16 mixed-precision quirks on MPS, keep batches modest (32 / 16), and set dataloader_pin_memory=False. Stage 1 is typically under 1–2 hours; Stage 2 is minutes; Naive Bayes is seconds.

Model: DistilBERT

Backbone: distilbert-base-uncased with a fresh 4-way sequence-classification head (AutoModelForSequenceClassification).

  • ~66M parameters — about 40% of BERT-base, trained by knowledge distillation.
  • Uncased WordPiece tokenizer; max sequence length capped at 64 tokens (headlines / short prefixes).
  • 4 labels: business, sport, tech, politics.
  • Classical baseline: Multinomial Naive Bayes + bag-of-words unigrams (CountVectorizer, min_df=2).

Lower-level architecture

DistilBERT is a compressed Transformer encoder. It keeps the BERT-style stack but drops the next-sentence prediction objective and halves the depth, then trains the student to match a BERT teacher.

Encoder stack

Input ids + attention mask │ ▼ Token + position embeddings (dim 768) │ ▼ 6 × Transformer blocks each: Multi-Head Self-Attention (12 heads) → residual + LayerNorm → Feed-Forward (768 → 3072 → 768) → residual + LayerNorm │ ▼ [CLS] pooled representation (768-d) │ ▼ Linear classification head 768 → 4 logits │ ▼ Softmax → predicted topic

What “fine-tuning” updates

We do full continued fine-tuning: every DistilBERT weight plus the classification head receive gradients. LoRA / adapters were not needed — the model is small, the UK set is small, and full updates finish quickly on MPS.

  • Forward: tokenize → pad/truncate to ≤64 → encoder → logits.
  • Loss: cross-entropy over the four labels.
  • Optimizer path: Hugging Face Trainer (AdamW-style), lr 2e-5, weight decay 0.01, seed 42.
  • Selection: best checkpoint by validation macro-F1.

Why DistilBERT (not a generative LLM)

This is single-label topic classification. An encoder with a linear head is the right inductive bias: cheap, stable, and easy to compare against a Naive Bayes bag-of-words baseline. We deliberately do not train a large generative model from scratch.

Fine-tuning techniques

The core method is two-stage domain adaptation (continued fine-tuning), with an ablated UK-only run to separate “UK data helps” from “AG pre-training still helps.”

1

Stage 1 — AG News titles (“before”)

Init from distilbert-base-uncased. Train on stratified AG titles (~28.5k train / 1.5k val). 2 epochs, batch 32. Save artifacts/model_ag_headlines/. Evaluate on frozen UK test.

2

Stage 2 — continue on UK (“after”)

Load the Stage-1 checkpoint (do not re-init from base). Train on UK train; early-stop on UK val (patience 1, up to 3 epochs), batch 16. Save artifacts/model_uk_finetuned/. Re-evaluate on the same UK test.

3

Ablation — UK only (no AG)

Fresh distilbert-base-uncased fine-tuned only on UK train/val with the same Stage-2 recipe. Isolates whether gains come from UK data alone vs AG→UK continued training.

4

Baselines — Multinomial Naive Bayes

Bag-of-words NB trained on AG titles, then separately on UK train; both scored on the same UK test. A cheap ceiling/floor check for the neural models.

Not used: LoRA, QLoRA, prefix-tuning, or frozen-encoder linear probes. Full updates are appropriate here; parameter-efficient methods would mostly add complexity without a clear win on this size of model and data.

Evaluation design

  • Frozen UK test — never used for training or early stopping.
  • Metrics: accuracy, macro-F1, micro-F1, per-class F1, confusion matrices.
  • Micro-F1 = accuracy here (single-label multi-class); both reported for lecture-style comparison.
  • Headline results: AG→UK fine-tuned reaches ~0.96 accuracy / macro-F1 on UK test; largest class gain vs AG-only is politics. Details on Classification and in RESULTS.md.

Side analyses dig into why transfer is hard: embedding centroid similarity, headline length distributions, and UK words missing from AG train.

Caveats & design risks

  • Label bridge World ↔ politics is noisy by construction.
  • BBC “headlines” are sometimes truncated article leads, not newsstand titles.
  • UK data is small — Stage 2 early-stops on val; over-training would memorize fast.
  • AG-only DistilBERT already beats NB @ AG; most of the big jump to ~0.95 comes from UK data itself, with AG→UK still edging UK-only.
